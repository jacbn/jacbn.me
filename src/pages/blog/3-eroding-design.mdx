# Designing in an eroding industry

<p class="text-muted"><em>\[This was originally posted on my [LinkedIn](https://lnkd.in/p/eRxxR3ei). I've copied it here for longevity.\]</em></p>

in a few days, i'll have quit my job at Isaac Science. it's been a great ride, and the level of support from the entire team has been truly heartwarming. it's an honour to have been so responsible for a website so widely loved* by so many across the country.

but fundamentally, i'm someone who thrives off passion. i want to lose myself in exploration, in creativity, in joy. i want to author an experience that genuinely stays with people, one that delights in all the small details others would ignore. and i want its implementation to be accessible to all who need it - by design, not painted on.

i'm struggling to reconcile this attention to detail with how the industry seems to be treating its users. attitudes like pushing barely-tested code in the name of velocity, in forgetting to do research, not looking at or undertaking studies - the human experience has been so universally eroded it's hard to feel welcome, both as a user and a designer/developer who cares. 

and yet, i'm remaining hopeful. i know there are people out there who work against the norm, who work with a keen focus. and if this is you - i'd love to connect.
